## The design logic of separation of powers

The US Constitution's authors were frightened of concentrated power. They distributed authority across three institutions — Congress, the Presidency, and the Judiciary — and further divided Congress into House and Senate with different term lengths, electorates, and sizes.

The underlying logic was that any group seeking to change policy would have to build consensus across multiple independent institutions. This was a deliberate choice to slow legislative action and protect against temporary majorities imposing permanent changes. It was also designed in a context of 13 states, an agrarian economy, and no concept of political parties.

The modern system has preserved the veto structure while the political context has changed dramatically. The result is a system where the default outcome on almost any contested issue is no change.

**The House of Representatives** — 435 members elected every two years in single-member districts. District boundaries are drawn by state legislatures in most states, creating strong incentives for partisan gerrymandering. A party that controls a state legislature after a census can draw maps that give it a structural advantage for the following decade.

**The Senate** — 100 members, two per state regardless of population. Wyoming (population 580,000) has the same Senate representation as California (population 39 million). This chamber significantly over-represents rural, less populous, and typically more conservative states relative to the national population.

**The Presidency** — elected via the Electoral College, not popular vote. In 2000 and 2016, the winner of the presidency lost the popular vote. The winner-take-all allocation of electoral votes in most states concentrates both campaigns on a handful of swing states.

> A bill can pass the House, fail to overcome a Senate filibuster, face a presidential veto, or be struck down by the Supreme Court. Each of these is a structurally independent obstacle.

## The filibuster problem

The filibuster is not in the Constitution. It emerged from a procedural accident in 1806 and evolved into a powerful tool for blocking legislation. In its modern form, it allows any senator to delay indefinitely — simply by declaring intent. Ending debate requires 60 votes (cloture). Since neither party has reliably held 60 Senate seats in recent decades, any determined minority can block virtually any legislation.

The filibuster's practical effect is that most major legislation requires 60 of 100 senators — a supermajority. Bills with majority support in both chambers, and majority public approval in polling, routinely die in the Senate. Universal background checks for gun purchases, for example, have polled above 80–90% support for decades while failing to pass the 60-vote threshold.

Budget reconciliation — a parliamentary procedure that bypasses the filibuster — is the primary mechanism for passing major legislation with a simple majority. But it can only be used once per budget year and has strict constraints on content.

## Campaign finance and who gets heard

The Supreme Court's 2010 Citizens United decision held that political spending is a form of protected free speech and that corporations, unions, and other organisations can spend unlimited amounts on independent political advertising. The 2014 McCutcheon decision removed limits on total individual donations to parties and candidates.

The practical result is that very large donors exercise disproportionate influence over which candidates receive funding, which races become competitive, and which policy positions candidates adopt. The political scientists Martin Gilens and Benjamin Page found in a 2014 study that US policy outcomes correlate strongly with the preferences of economic elites and organised interest groups, and weakly with the preferences of average citizens — even controlling for the possibility that elite and mass preferences sometimes overlap.

This does not mean ordinary voters are irrelevant. It means the set of candidates and positions that reach competitive viability is filtered before voting happens.
