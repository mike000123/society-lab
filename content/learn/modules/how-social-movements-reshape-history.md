## Big picture
The value of comparing movement families is that it stops history from looking magical. Change does not happen only because a heroic leader appears or because society suddenly becomes enlightened. Repeatedly, movements grow when a grievance can be shared, framed, communicated, organized, and pushed into a moment where the existing order loses legitimacy.

What changes across eras is the medium. Print made doctrine and authority easier to challenge. Industrial networks helped scale unions, parties, and national campaigns. Broadcast media changed the politics of witness. Digital networks accelerated the spread of testimony and coordination. But in every era, organization still decides whether the moment becomes durable.

> The long arc lesson is that technology opens the door, but political structure decides how far the movement gets once the door is open.

## What the evidence layer shows
The attached evidence pages help explain why movement history accelerates over time.

- Human-rights and women's-rights trends show that the boundaries of recognized citizenship have been contested and expanded, not simply gifted from above.
- Global education matters because literacy and mass schooling widen the pool of people who can read, organize, interpret events, and participate in public argument.
- Internet diffusion matters because it dramatically changes how quickly a new frame can move from one locality into a wider public.

## The recurring sequence
The broad sequence is often: grievance becomes visible, a frame names the injustice, a medium spreads the frame, organizations recruit and coordinate, disruption raises the cost of normal rule, repression may deepen the legitimacy crisis, and institutions are forced to concede, adapt, or crack.

Not every movement completes that sequence. Some never build durable organization. Some face overwhelming repression. Some win symbolic visibility without material change. But the pattern is useful because it lets learners ask why one movement family breaks through while another stalls.

## What to remember about historical outcomes
Movement victories are never pure endings. They open new stages. Independence can arrive without full decolonization. Rights can be recognized without full access. Digital mobilization can change public language faster than institutions move.

That is exactly why a timeline and simulator belong together. The timeline helps users see the recurring mechanics across eras, and the simulator helps them test which conditions usually matter most when history bends.
